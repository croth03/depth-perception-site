03 / AI DISCUSSION

Critical design dialogue

AI proposed a structure. Critique made it a concept.

The useful outcome was not the first answer. It was the sequence of proposing, checking, rejecting, researching, and rewriting until the mechanic and theme supported each other.

Three iterations

The names reveal how the thinking changed.

START / PROTOTYPE

Background · Middle · Foreground

Mechanically accurate but emotionally neutral. The planes described where the character was, not what the experience meant.

Useful for collision and UI; insufficient as a game concept.
AI PROPOSAL

Seen · Remembered · Imagined

More thematic, but too categorical. It risked treating complex mental experiences as neat, fixed boxes.

Provocative language; weak fit with the desired uncertainty.
CRITICAL REVISION

Reality · Hallucination · Lost Reality

A progression of certainty rather than a claim about distinct psychological zones.

Supports escalation while leaving interpretation open.

Research checks

Claims about perception needed sources—not atmosphere.

CHECK 01 / VARIATION

Hallucination is not one visual style.

Clinical reviews describe varied perceptual experiences. That ruled out presenting neon glitch as a universal shorthand.

Review source ↗
CHECK 02 / TERMS

Illusion and hallucination differ.

An illusion misinterprets something present; a hallucination is generally described without corresponding external stimulus. The game uses both as design ideas without claiming clinical simulation.

Perception framework ↗
CHECK 03 / AMBIGUITY

Uncertain forms invite projection.

Pareidolia research supported fog, shadow, and partial silhouettes as better material than random visual noise.

Ambiguous-form source ↗

Gaps identified

Three tempting ideas that did not survive review.

GAP 01

Categories felt too certain.

Seen / Remembered / Imagined explained too much and suggested a psychology the game could not responsibly or meaningfully simulate.

GAP 02

Confusion looked like bugs.

Invisible platforms and unclear depth were frustrating in the prototype. Uncertainty needed authored tells and consistent consequences.

GAP 03

Glitch was generic.

Random distortion would communicate “digital error,” not the more interesting experience of sincerely doubting what was seen.

Selected exchange · summarized

The productive moment was disagreement.

AI

Use three planes as the Seen, the Remembered, and the Imagined.

DESIGNER

That feels too literal. I want fog, vague figures, false platforms, and a recurring door—evidence that can be interpreted more than one way.

AI

Then organize the arc by changing perceptual certainty, not by fixed mental categories.

DESIGNER

Yes—but the world can become unreliable only if control and collision remain dependable.

Final design decision

Reality → Hallucination → Lost Reality

The stages are an authored dramatic progression, not diagnoses. The work remains original because the final structure, aesthetic boundaries, rule set, and selected output came through human evaluation and revision—not automatic acceptance.

AI

Capability + limitation

Fast divergence; weak accountability.

AI was effective at generating alternate structures, summarizing options, and visualizing a coherent direction quickly. It was less reliable at judging whether an idea was fair, original enough, sensitive, or actually fun. Hallucination mitigation required source checking, explicit credits, separating fact from proposal, and testing every generated claim against the project’s design rules. Human curation decided what to reject, what to rewrite, and what belonged in the final game.
